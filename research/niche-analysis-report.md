# Industry Benchmark Report — Romanian Photovoltaic Installation Market

*Prepared for: SAMGEC SRL · Satu Mare · 12 May 2026*

---

## Executive Summary
The Romanian residential photovoltaic market is in a structural growth phase, driven by the AFM **Casa Verde Fotovoltaice** subsidy program and the legislated prosumator framework (ANRE). Buyer intent is **high**, but customer journey friction is concentrated in two places: (1) the ANRE dossier and racordare paperwork, and (2) trust in the installer to handle that paperwork correctly.

The companies winning today are **not** the ones with the cheapest panels — they are the ones who frame themselves as paperwork-handlers, quantify the household economy in lei/euro, and surface authorizations as the trust foundation.

SAMGEC currently holds **more** authorizations than the average installer ranking on Google's first page, but is **not surfacing** them effectively. The current site scored 28/80 on our 8-criteria audit; the average top-5 competitor scored 62/80.

---

## Method
We searched Google.ro for the queries Romanian buyers use to find PV installers (`firma panouri fotovoltaice`, `instalare panouri fotovoltaice`, `Casa Verde fotovoltaice`), shortlisted the top 10 results, and scored each on 8 criteria: search visibility, public review quality, visual design, mobile responsiveness, content depth, social proof, CTA strategy, page speed. We then deep-scraped the top 5 to extract design patterns and messaging strategy.

## Five Patterns of the Top 10%

1. **Hero promises a euro outcome, not a slogan.** Every leading site opens with a quantified bill reduction (70–100%) and a payback period (5–7 years). SAMGEC's current hero ("Mulți fac instalații. Doar că noi le facem foarte bine") communicates pride but commits to no measurable buyer outcome.

2. **The paperwork is reframed as "we handle it."** Edvari, Engie, and Genway all dedicate a major homepage section to ANRE dossier, racordare avization, and bidirectional meter coordination. This is the single largest objection a Romanian PV buyer holds. The installer that owns it visibly wins the call.

3. **Hardware brands are named explicitly.** Longi, Canadian Solar, Jinko, JA Solar, Huawei, Deye, SolarEdge, Sungrow, Victron. Naming brands does double duty: SEO (buyers Google brand names) and trust (validates premium tier).

4. **The process is a numbered timeline.** Edvari's nine-step timeline from first consultation to grid connection — each with a duration — was the strongest conversion element we observed. It closes the "is this complicated?" objection.

5. **Authorizations are surfaced as a badge wall, not buried in a sub-page.** ANRE, ISCIR, ISO 9001/14001/18001, and AFM agreed-installer status all appear prominently on top-ranked competitor homepages.

| **30-year history** | SAMGEC predates 95% of PV installers in the market. Most competitors were founded post-2015. This is a defensible trust signal. |
| **Multi-trade depth** | Buyers often want PV **plus** a heat-pump, **plus** electrical work. SAMGEC can quote all three; most PV-only specialists cannot. |
| **AFM Casa Verde agreed status** | High commercial intent keyword (`Casa Verde fotovoltaice Satu Mare`) currently has weak local results. SAMGEC can own it. |

## Recommended Approach
1. Rebuild on a static, fast HTML/CSS/GSAP stack — leave WordPress/Elementor behind. Expected speed improvement: 3–4× faster TTFB.
2. Lead the homepage with quantified PV economy + ANRE-handled framing.
3. Build a dedicated PV pillar page targeting `panouri fotovoltaice Satu Mare` with LocalBusiness schema.
4. Surface the full authorization stack on the homepage trust strip.
5. Add an 8-step process timeline and 3 representative system case studies (with kWp + €/year).
6. Add a 10-question FAQ with schema.org markup for rich snippets.
7. Replace all current Lorem Ipsum placeholder content (Casa Verde page).
